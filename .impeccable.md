## Design Context

### Users

Casual players using a desktop browser with keyboard and mouse. They want the wonder of flying over recognizable real places without needing prior aviation knowledge or learning a study-level simulator. The primary job is to choose a real aircraft and starting location, get into the air quickly, sightsee, and land with enough authentic control to feel like flying.

### Brand Personality

Cinematic, approachable, authentic. The experience should create awe through scenery and aircraft presence while remaining calm, legible, and forgiving. Aviation detail should support immersion rather than become a barrier.

### Aesthetic Direction

A light, aviation-inspired interface laid over a high-fidelity 3D world. The scenery and aircraft remain the visual focus, with restrained controls resembling contemporary cockpit documentation rather than a game dashboard. Avoid neon sci-fi styling, dark glassmorphism, dense study-simulator panels, cartoon presentation, and generic card-grid menus.

### Design Principles

1. Put the world first: maximize the visible landscape and keep interface chrome quiet.
2. Authentic where visible, simplified where burdensome: real aircraft, airports, terrain, and essential controls without procedural cockpit overhead.
3. Make flight recoverable: teach through immediate feedback, optional assistance, rewind, and runway reset rather than punishment.
4. Reveal complexity gradually: start with a clean flight view and expose maps, instruments, and assistance only when needed.
5. Protect smoothness: stable frame pacing, responsive controls, and graceful scenery detail reduction matter more than maximal density.

### Initial Product Constraints

- Desktop web application.
- Keyboard and mouse input for milestone one.
- Light interface.
- Initial region: Oʻahu, Hawaii.
- Aircraft: Cessna 172, Daher TBM 930, Icon A5, and Airbus A380.
- Visible 3D cockpit with essential interactive controls and an elevated external chase camera.
- Hand-tuned clear daytime conditions for the first milestone.
- Forgiving five-second rewind and reset-to-runway recovery.
- Free/open geographic data and self-hosted assets; no Cesium or Cesium ion dependency.
